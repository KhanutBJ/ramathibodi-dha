# Curriculum overview

This is the map. The Academy teaches medical AI as one craft, from the first
line of code to a tool a hospital can trust. You do not need to be a programmer
to start. You do need to be willing to build.

The curriculum is organised into six domains and two pathways, then a capstone.
Most people move through the domains in order, then pick a pathway that matches
where they want their work to land.

## Who this is for, and what you need first

```{note}
**Prerequisites for the whole programme**
- A laptop and a Google account. Everything runs in the browser or in Google Colab.
- Comfort with clinical language. You do not need maths beyond hospital statistics.
- No prior coding. Domain 1 (Basics) assumes you have never written a line.
- About 4 to 6 hours a week if you are self-paced, more in a cohort week.
```

The Academy is built for clinicians, students, nurses, engineers, and scientists.
If you can hold a clinical problem in your head, you can start here.

## How the programme is structured

Two ways to take it, same curriculum:

- **Self-paced.** Open any domain and work through it at your own speed. Every
  domain is free and ends in something you built, not a quiz.
- **Cohort.** A guided run of the curriculum with a group, on a fixed calendar,
  with live sessions and a shared project. Cohorts open with each intake.

The full programme is **12 weeks** in cohort form: six domains over eight weeks,
a pathway over two weeks, and a capstone over the final two.

## The weekly rhythm

Every week of a domain follows the same four-beat rhythm. It is deliberately
close to how Neuromatch Academy runs, because it works: you learn a little, you
do it immediately, you build, then you are reviewed.

| Beat | Format | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Short written lesson | 45 to 60 min | The concept, in plain language, with clinical examples. |
| **Tutorial** | Guided notebook (Colab) | 90 min | You run and modify real code. Nothing to install. |
| **Build** | Independent task | 2 to 3 hrs | You apply it to a small problem from your own work. |
| **Review** | Pod or self-check | 45 min | Feedback from a pod, a mentor, or the module checklist. |

In a cohort, the Review beat is a live **pod**: four to six people who move
through the programme together and read each other's work. Self-paced learners
use the "Check yourself" checklist at the end of each module instead.

## The six domains

| # | Domain | Prerequisite | Time | Sessions | You leave able to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Basics** | None | ~8 to 10 hrs | 5 | Set up a workshop and ship a tiny tool without fear |
| 2 | **AI Agent** | Basics | ~10 to 12 hrs | 6 | Build a supervised assistant that does real work |
| 3 | **Deep AI** | Basics | ~12 to 14 hrs | 6 | Train and interpret a model on real medical data |
| 4 | **Digital Health** | Basics | ~10 to 12 hrs | 6 | Work fluently with the data a Thai hospital holds |
| 5 | **Deployment** | Domains 2 to 4 | ~8 to 10 hrs | 4 | Put a model in front of users and measure it |
| 6 | **Strategy and Governance** | Domain 5 | ~6 to 8 hrs | 4 | Take a tool toward something approvable |

Each domain page opens with its own prerequisites, time, objectives, a
session-by-session outline, and a "Check yourself" list. Read that header before
you start the domain, the way you would read the agenda before a workshop.

## The two pathways

After the six domains, pick one, two weeks each:

- **Startup pathway.** For people who want their work to become a product.
  Validation, the move from research to venture, and the road through the
  National Innovation Agency and the Thai HealthTech ecosystem.
- **Hospital information pathway.** For people who want to strengthen the system
  from inside. Interoperability, data governance, and AI that fits a real
  clinical workflow.

## The capstone

The programme ends where the club begins: you build one real thing. Over two
weeks you take a genuine clinical problem, build a tool, evaluate it honestly
with its denominator and its limits, and present it for review. This is the
artefact that goes in your portfolio and, at its best, into a pilot.

## How we teach, and how you progress

Every domain ends in something you built, not a quiz. You read, you run, you
build, in that order. Notebooks open in your browser or in Google Colab.

You progress by finishing artefacts, not by passing tests. Each domain adds one
piece to a portfolio you carry into the Fellowship, the Platform, or a job. The
Learning Navigator can map your next step from where you are.

We hold to the standard set by accredited clinician-facing education such as the
AMA Ed Hub course *Practical Applications for AI in Health Care*, then add the
part most courses leave out: you make the thing, under supervision, with
governance built in from the start.
