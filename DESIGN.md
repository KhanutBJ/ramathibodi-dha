# Design language

The name of our design language is **Clinical Light**.

It comes from one idea: the work is serious, so the surface should be calm. A
hospital is loud, fluorescent, and crowded. Our screens are the opposite. They
give the eye room to think. The restraint is not a style choice. It is respect
for people who do hard work and do not need a website shouting at them.

Three lineages meet here, and we owe each of them.

- **IBM Carbon** gives us discipline: a grid, a type scale, components that
  behave. Nothing is arbitrary.
- **Dieter Rams** gives us subtraction: as little design as possible, every
  element earning its place. "Good design is as little design as possible."
- **Apple under Jony Ive** gives us clarity and material honesty: deep type,
  real hierarchy, motion that explains rather than decorates.

What makes it ours, not a copy of those, is the soul underneath.

## The soul

**A single line, drawn once.** The logo is a brain drawn as one continuous,
looping line, the way a confident hand sketches an idea without lifting the pen.
That is the feeling we build everywhere: one clear gesture, drawn with intent.
The gradient that runs along it, orange to indigo, is the only place we let
colour sing. Everywhere else, ink and paper.

**Warm rigor.** Carbon and Rams can feel cold. We are a club born in a Thai
hospital, made of people who care for patients. So the geometry is precise but
the voice is human, the corners are softened, the light is warm. Rigor you can
sit next to.

**Earned colour.** The brand gradient is a reward, not wallpaper. It appears once
per view at most: a single word in a headline, one primary button, the brain
mark. When everything could glow, nothing means anything. Scarcity is what makes
the colour feel like a heartbeat.

**The bedside, not the lab.** Imagery and language stay close to care. We say
"to the bedside," not "end to end." The work is measured in patients helped.

## The rules

1. **Two surfaces, one system.** The public site and the Academy reader share the
   same tokens, type, and spacing. Learning should feel like the same place as
   the front door.
2. **Light and dark are equals.** Neither is an afterthought. The default follows
   the reader's system, and the toggle is always one tap away.
3. **Type is the design.** Montserrat for the voice (it is in our logo), Inter
   for the body, IBM Plex Mono for labels and code. Big, tight headlines. Generous,
   readable prose. The page is mostly words, set well.
4. **Whitespace is content.** Sections breathe. We would rather cut a paragraph
   than crowd one.
5. **Motion with a reason.** Things fade up as you reach them, once. No carousels,
   no parallax theatre. Motion confirms you arrived, then gets out of the way.
6. **Honesty over polish.** We show the denominator, the confidence interval, the
   population a model was tested on. Trust is a design material.
7. **No slop.** No em dashes, no middle dots, no filler. Human prose, or nothing.

## Why it has soul

Soul is not an effect you add at the end. It is the result of a point of view
held consistently. Ours: medicine is serious and people are tired, so be calm, be
clear, be warm, and let one line and one gradient carry the feeling. Do that
everywhere, and the site stops looking designed and starts feeling inevitable.
